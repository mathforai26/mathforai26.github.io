## What homework is for

Homework serves three purposes:

1. **Practice:** reconstruct definitions and proofs until the ideas become usable.
2. **Diagnosis:** discover gaps in prerequisites or understanding while there is time to repair them.
3. **Communication:** explain mathematical claims precisely, including assumptions and limits.

A typical two-week assignment will have about four substantial problems, usually with several parts. Some problems will develop one result in stages. Others will ask for a counterexample, a theorem audit, or a short explanation connecting a mathematical model to a current AI system.

## Submission format

Use the [homework LaTeX template]({{ '/documents/misc_files/homework_template.tex' | relative_url }}). Submit each graded homework through [Canvas Assignments](https://canvas.ualberta.ca/courses/37231/assignments) as a ZIP archive named `hwNN-ccid.zip`, where `NN` is the two-digit homework number and `ccid` is your University of Alberta CCID. For example: `hw01-jsmith.zip`.

The archive must contain a single top-level directory named `hwNN-ccid`. This directory must contain at least:

- `hwNN-ccid.pdf`: the submitted solution;
- `hwNN-ccid.tex`: the main LaTeX source.

Include any additional files needed to compile or reproduce the submission, such as figures, code, or data. Do not include generated LaTeX files such as `.aux`, `.log`, or `.synctex.gz`.

Your name and CCID must appear on the first page of the PDF. Identify collaborators and list all sources and tools used. The mathematical writing must be your own. The most recent archive submitted before the deadline will be graded.
