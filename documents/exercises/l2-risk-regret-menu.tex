\begin{exercisechunk}
\item \textbf{Changing the decision menu.}
\label[exercise]{ex:risk-regret-menu}
\exerciseprofile{2}{2}{2}{\exproof\quad\excalculation\quad\exinterpretation}{%
Explain why adding an unselected decision can change minimax regret but not
existing worst risks.}
Consider two decisions with risks
\[
R(A,1)=0.1,\quad R(A,2)=1.0,\qquad
R(B,1)=0.3,\quad R(B,2)=0.9.
\]
Here regret is measured relative to the smallest risk among the available
decisions in each environment. Determine whether adding an available decision
can change which of $A$ and $B$ is preferred under each criterion, even if the
new decision is not chosen.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item Show that adding a decision cannot reverse the minimax-risk ranking
of $A$ and $B$.

\item Compute the worst-case regret of each of $A$, $B$, and $C$ after adding
$C$, with risks $1.0$ and $0.05$. Identify the minimax-regret decision and
compare it with the choice in \cref{ex:risk-versus-regret}(a)(i).
\item Prove that adding a decision that improves neither environment's
best achievable risk leaves all existing regrets unchanged.
\end{enumerate}
\end{exercisechunk}
